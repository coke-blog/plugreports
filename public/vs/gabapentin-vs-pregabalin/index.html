Gabapentin vs Pregabalin

Gabapentin (Neurontin) and pregabalin (Lyrica) are sibling 'gabapentinoid' nerve-pain drugs — among the most prescribed medications on earth. Both calm over-firing nerves; both are increasingly abused for their calm, floaty high; and pregabalin is the stronger, faster, more abusable of the two.

The verdict

Pregabalin is gabapentin with the brakes removed: better absorption, faster onset, six times the potency, and a Schedule V label acknowledging its street value. Both are genuine medicines that transformed nerve-pain treatment — and both became drugs of misuse because their side-effect profile at high doses is a mild, warm dissociation. The deadliest fact about both isn't their solo profile: it's that they multiply opioid respiratory depression, and that combination fills morgues in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Appalachia alike.

?Frequently asked questions

Is pregabalin stronger than gabapentin?

Yes — roughly six times per milligram, with far more predictable absorption. Gabapentin's absorption actually saturates at high doses, which is why it needs megadosing to feel anything.

Is gabapentin addictive?

Yes, though less than pregabalin. Daily high-dose use produces tolerance and a real withdrawal (anxiety, insomnia, rebound pain). It's rarely scheduled federally precisely because solo overdose is uncommon — the danger is in combination.

Fentanyl vs Heroin

Updated 2026-09-21Sources: CDC, NIDA, DEA

Heroin ruled the illegal opioid world for a century. Fentanyl — 50 times stronger, made in labs, shipped by mail — replaced it in under a decade. Today, heroin barely exists in North American street supplies that haven't been taken over by fentanyl or its analogues.

The verdict

This is the comparison that changed drug history. Fentanyl's strength means the gap between high and dead is measured in milligrams; its presence in counterfeit pills and as heroin adulterant means people overdose without ever choosing fentanyl. Heroin's slower, longer withdrawal is more survivable — fentanyl's overdose is faster and needs more naloxone. If you use street opioids, assume fentanyl, carry naloxone, never use alone, and test with strips.

?Frequently asked questions

Is fentanyl the same as heroin?

No. Heroin comes from the opium poppy; fentanyl is fully synthetic and 50 times stronger. Dealers add fentanyl to heroin or sell it as heroin because it's cheaper per dose.

Why are fentanyl overdoses so hard to reverse?

Fentanyl acts fast and clears fast, so naloxone can wear off before the person is safe — repeat dosing and calling an ambulance are essential. High-potency mixes (nitazenes) may need many doses.

Vapes vs Cigarettes

Vapes and cigarettes deliver the same drug — nicotine — through very different machinery. Cigarettes burn tobacco and deliver nicotine with tar, carbon monoxide and 70 known carcinogens. Vapes heat liquid and deliver nicotine with far fewer toxins. Public health bodies now largely agree: for a smoker, switching to vaping is a harm-reduction win; for a non-smoker, starting vaping is a harm.

The verdict

For the smoker choosing between the two, the evidence pyramid points one way: switching fully to vaping reduces toxicant exposure by a huge margin, and England's NHS literally prescribes vapes as quitting tools. For the never-smoker — especially the teenager — the equation inverts: nicotine addiction, unknown long-term aerosol effects, and a teen vaping epidemic justify real caution. The honest public-health frame: vapes are for smokers who can't quit otherwise, not a new habit for a new generation.

?Frequently asked questions

Are vapes safer than cigarettes?

For smokers who switch completely, UK public health bodies estimate vaping is around 95% less harmful. That doesn't make it safe — it makes it less dangerous than the deadliest product on earth.

Can vapes help you quit smoking?

Yes — they're now the most-used quitting aid in several countries, and clinical trials show they outperform nicotine patches for many smokers. Dual use (smoking AND vaping) keeps most of the harm, though.

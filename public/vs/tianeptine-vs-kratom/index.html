Tianeptine and kratom are the two gray-market substances most often bought for opioid-like relief: tianeptine ('gas-station heroin') as a supplement, kratom as a leaf. Tianeptine is a prescription antidepressant in Europe that hits opioid receptors directly; kratom is a plant with a milder, multi-alkaloid effect.

The verdict

Tianeptine is the sharper knife: a genuine opioid sold next to energy drinks, with genuine opioid dependence arriving on a month's schedule. Kratom's plant matrix builds in a ceiling that tianeptine lacks — leaf alone has never been shown to stop breathing, while tianeptine has deaths on the record. Several US states have already banned tianeptine; the trajectory suggests kratom survives regulation and tianeptine doesn't.

?Frequently asked questions

Is tianeptine an opioid?

Functionally yes — it agonizes mu-opioid receptors directly, and high doses produce a classic opioid high and withdrawal. It's prescribed at low doses in Europe as an antidepressant with a different (non-opioid-forward) effect.

Why is tianeptine sold in gas stations?

A supplement-law loophole: it's an old, unpatented drug never marketed as a US dietary ingredient, yet enforcement lagged for years while 'Zaza Red' built a national addiction footprint. States are closing the gap one ban at a time.

Ketamine vs PCP

Updated 2026-09-21Sources: NIDA, FDA

Ketamine and PCP are chemical siblings — both NMDA-receptor dissociatives discovered by the same lab family in the 1960s. Ketamine became the world's safest anesthetic and a groundbreaking depression treatment. PCP became the media's monster drug. The truth is more nuanced: same mechanism, different dose curves and reputations.

The verdict

History picked its winner: ketamine's shorter, gentler dissociation made it medicine; PCP's longer, heavier trip made it a byword. Both can produce the K-hole/PCP-hole state; ketamine just gets you there and back in an hour. Ketamine's own dangers are real — bladder destruction in daily users, dependence, and fatal accidents when mixed with depressants — but the drug-treatment world now views ketamine as therapy while PCP remains a cautionary tale.

?Frequently asked questions

Is ketamine the same as PCP?

Same receptor, same family tree — ketamine was literally derived as a safer PCP alternative. Different durations, different histories, overlapping states of mind.

Is PCP really that dangerous?

PCP's violence reputation is heavily exaggerated by media; its real harms are accidents during long dissociation and the steep dose curve. That said, its long duration makes bad experiences much harder to ride out than ketamine's.

Ketamine vs DXM

Ketamine is the pharmaceutical dissociative. DXM (dextromethorphan) is the cough-syrup dissociative — legal, cheap, and sitting in every pharmacy. Both hit the NMDA receptor; both produce floating, out-of-body states. One comes with a doctor's dosing; the other comes with a label warning most users ignore.

The verdict

Ketamine is the cleaner, shorter, more controllable dissociative with a real medical pedigree. DXM is the people's dissociative — legal, affordable, but dirty: combo-syrup ingredients add organ toxicity, antidepressant interactions can trigger serotonin syndrome, and the trip lasts three times as long. Teenagers who can't access ketamine robotrip instead; the ER visits usually involve the bottle's other ingredients.

?Frequently asked questions

Is DXM like ketamine?

Same receptor family, similar dissociation — users compare robotripping to a longer, dirtier K-hole with more body weirdness.

Is DXM dangerous?

Pure DXM in reasonable doses is physically safe for most — the dangers are combo-syrup ingredients, antidepressant interactions (serotonin syndrome), and doing it around cars, water or balconies.

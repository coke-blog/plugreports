Kratom vs 7-OH

Updated 2026-09-21Sources: FDA, DEA, NIDA

Kratom leaf and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) come from the same plant — but they are not the same product. Leaf is the traditional, milder form; 7-OH is the concentrated alkaloid, sold in gas-station shots and tablets, that acts far more like a prescription opioid and is now facing federal scheduling.

The verdict

7-OH is kratom's main active ingredient distilled into pill form — it removes everything that made leaf 'gentle' (low concentration, slow onset) and keeps everything that makes opioids dangerous (fast, strong, compulsive). Poison-center calls and dependence reports track the concentrates, not the tea. If you use kratom, leaf and known doses are the lower-risk form; 7-OH shots are the closest thing to a legal pill opioid on a gas-station counter.

?Frequently asked questions

Is 7-OH the same as kratom?

Chemically, 7-OH is one of kratom's alkaloids. Commercially it's a different product — extracted or synthesized and sold in concentrated form, with effects much closer to traditional opioids.

Why is the government banning 7-OH but not kratom?

Because concentrated 7-OH behaves like a potent opioid while leaf has a built-in ceiling from its alkaloid mix. Regulators are targeting the concentrated form first.

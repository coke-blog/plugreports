Mixing dangers

Mixing drugs — why combinations kill more than any single drug

Here is the number most people never hear: most fatal overdoses involve more than one substance. Mixing is not additive — it is multiplicative. Two depressants together can stop your breathing at doses either one would survive alone. A stimulant masks a downer’s warning signs until it is too late. And medicines mixed without a doctor’s oversight — a friend’s prescription, a pill from a stranger, a drink on top of a sleep aid — are the single most common path into an overdose. These 61 plain-language guides explain exactly why each combination is dangerous, what the mix does to your body, the warning signs of trouble, and what to do in the first minutes.

If you’re mixing or thinking about it, read the deadly tier first.Those combinations kill quickly — often before help can arrive. If someone is unresponsive or breathing slowly, call emergency services now, then give naloxone if opioids might be involved. Hotlines

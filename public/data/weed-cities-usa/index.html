Top 10 US cities with the most weed use

The US cities where cannabis use is highest — ranked from NSDUH state surveys, dispensary-density data and market reports, from Portland's weed saturation to Oklahoma City's dispensary boom.

~15,000cannabis dispensaries across the US — 79% of Americans live in a county with at least one (Pew, 2024)
36dispensaries per 100,000 residents in Oklahoma — the highest density of any state (Pew, 2024)
24states where recreational cannabis is legal — plus medical in most of the rest

How this ranking works

No US agency publishes a per-city weed use ranking, so this list triangulates three proxies: SAMHSA's National Survey on Drug Use and Health (which publishes state-level past-month cannabis use), dispensary counts and density (tracked by Pew Research and state regulators), and legal-market sales data. They point at a consistent map: the highest-use cities sit in the legal West and Northeast, but the most saturated retail market in the country is in medical-only Oklahoma. The ranking is directional — treat the middle of the list as a band, not an order.

#CityWhy it ranks here
1Portland, OROregon consistently posts among the highest past-month adult cannabis use rates in NSDUH state tables, and an early industry analysis found Oregon leading the nation in dispensaries per capita (16.5 per 100,000 residents). Cheap legal flower and a mature market keep Portland at the top of every proxy.
2Denver, COThe original legal-weed city (retail since 2014). Colorado remains in the top tier of NSDUH past-month use, and Denver's metro hosts hundreds of dispensaries plus the country's most established consumption culture.
3Oklahoma City, OKThe strangest weed market in America: medical-only, but with effectively no license cap, Oklahoma reached roughly 36 dispensaries per 100,000 residents — the highest density in the nation (Pew, 2024). OKC and Tulsa anchor a market where weed is easier to buy than in most fully legal states.
4Los Angeles, CALos Angeles County alone had 1,481 dispensaries at Pew's 2024 count — more than any state except California itself. Add an enormous legacy market and LA is the largest weed city in the country by sheer volume.
5Burlington, VTVermont regularly tops or nears the top of NSDUH past-month use tables, especially among young adults. Burlington — a small, young, college-heavy city — concentrates that statewide pattern.
6Seattle / Tacoma, WAWashington legalized alongside Colorado in 2012, and the Puget Sound region shows durable top-tier use rates in federal surveys, with one of the country's densest retail networks.
7San Francisco / Oakland, CAThe Bay Area's cannabis culture predates legalization by decades — Oakland issued the first dispensary permits in 2004. High use rates meet high prices and a still-active legacy market.
8Detroit, MIMichigan has grown into one of America's largest legal markets (multi-billion-dollar annual sales, among the highest per-capita in the Midwest), and Detroit's metro plus its cheap flower prices drive the state's numbers.
9Anchorage, AKAlaska posts some of the highest past-month use rates in NSDUH tables year after year; Anchorage holds most of the state's population and nearly all of its retail market.
10Las Vegas, NVA high-use resident market amplified by ~40 million tourists a year — Nevada's per-capita sales rank among the highest in the country, and dispensary density around the Strip is extreme.

Also worth knowing

Honorable mentions: Boston and Worcester (Massachusetts' mature market), Chicago (Illinois' high-tax boom), Ann Arbor (home of Hash Bash since 1972), and Tucson/Phoenix (Arizona's fast-growing market). Notably absent: New York City — huge in absolute terms, but middling per-capita, with a legal rollout slowed by enforcement against unlicensed shops. EUDA wastewater comparisons find US and Canadian cities carry higher THC metabolite loads than even the highest European cities.

Methodology

Methodology: composite of SAMHSA NSDUH state-level past-month cannabis use (most recent tables), dispensary counts and per-capita density (Pew Research 2024 analysis of state licensing data; Cannabiz Media licensing counts), and state market-sales reports. No city-level use survey exists; rankings are directional.

Sources

  • Pew Research Center: 'Most Americans now live in a legal marijuana state' (2024)
  • State cannabis regulatory sales reports (CO, OR, MI, NV, OK)

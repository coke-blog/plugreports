Top 10 Canadian cities with the most weed use

Where weed use is highest in Canada — Halifax leads the wastewater ranking, Montreal and the BC coast follow, per Statistics Canada's wastewater pilot and National Cannabis Survey.

How this ranking works

Canada has something almost no other country does: a federal agency that measured city-level cannabis use in sewage. Statistics Canada's wastewater pilot tested five major metros, and its National Cannabis Survey adds provincial per-capita consumption. Together they draw a clear map — Atlantic Canada and the West Coast lead, the big Ontario and Quebec metros sit in the middle, and usage everywhere is high by global standards: European wastewater comparisons find Canadian cities' THC metabolite loads well above the highest EU cities. Treat positions 4–8 as a band.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures weed use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Halifax, NSThe surprise champion: Halifax wastewater carried 1,310 micrograms of THC metabolite per person per week — the highest of any metro StatCan tested — and Nova Scotia posts the highest provincial consumption (27.1 g per person per year). A young, student-heavy city in a high-use province.
2Montreal, QCSecond in the wastewater pilot at 976 µg per person per week — despite Quebec having the country's strictest retail rules (government stores, 21+ age limit). Montreal's huge student population keeps it near the top.
3Victoria, BCBritish Columbia ties for the highest provincial consumption outside Atlantic Canada (24.1 g per person per year), and BC survey data consistently places the capital region among the province's highest-use areas — a legacy of the coast's decades-old cannabis culture.
4Toronto, ONMiddle of the wastewater table (451 µg) but the country's largest absolute market — Toronto's dispensary count exploded after Ontario's retail expansion, and past-year use in the metro tracks the national average of a high-use country.
5Edmonton, ABClose behind Toronto in the wastewater pilot (416 µg), and Alberta ties BC near the top of provincial consumption (24.6 g per person per year). Alberta also built the country's most competitive private retail market.
6Vancouver, BCThe spiritual home of Canadian weed — but only 288 µg per person per week in the wastewater pilot, the lowest of the five metros tested. The likely explanation: BC's mature legacy market means heavy users, but the city's overall rate is diluted by demographics. Culture ≠ volume.
7Calgary, ABShares Alberta's top-tier provincial numbers; Calgary and Edmonton function as one high-consumption corridor split by 300 km of highway.
8Kelowna, BCInterior BC posts some of the province's highest use rates in regional surveys — the Okanagan's grow-country heritage shows up in the data, and Kelowna concentrates it.
9Ottawa, ONNational-average use but one of the country's densest dispensary maps after Ontario's open licensing — Ottawa's ByWard Market area became a notorious dispensary cluster.
10Winnipeg, MBManitoba sits mid-table provincially, but Winnipeg's young demographics and harsh winters (indoor drug) keep it in the top ten across proxies.

Also worth knowing

StatCan's wastewater pilot (2018) covered only five metros — Halifax, Montreal, Toronto, Edmonton and Vancouver — so cities beyond those rely on provincial survey data. Since legalization, national past-year use has crept upward while the legal market's share has grown past the illicit one; provincial patterns (Atlantic Canada and BC/Alberta high, Quebec mid despite strict rules) have stayed stable.

Methodology

Methodology: Statistics Canada wastewater-based epidemiology pilot (THC metabolite loads, five metros, 2018), StatCan National Cannabis Survey provincial consumption estimates, and provincial retail data. Where no city measurement exists, provincial rates and market density are used as proxies. Rankings are directional.

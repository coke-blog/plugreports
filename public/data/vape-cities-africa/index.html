Top 10 African cities with the most vaping

Africa's vaping map — South Africa's R1.5-billion legal market leads the continent, from Cape Town's vape lounges to Lagos's youth boom, per market and survey data.

How this ranking works

African vaping is really two stories: South Africa — a formal, taxed, retail-driven market worth over R3 billion and home to the continent's only dedicated vape tax — and everywhere else, where cheap disposables spread through informal trade faster than regulators can react. No African country publishes city-level vaping data, so this ranking combines market research, the University of Cape Town's youth studies, and national policy reporting. The pattern: English-speaking, middle-class, urban Africa vapes; the market follows the money. Every position is an estimate.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures vaping use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Cape Town, ZAThe continent's vaping capital: the densest concentration of dedicated vape shops in Africa, a visible lounge and 'cloud' culture, and the university studies (UCT) that documented the youth boom — over a quarter of high-school seniors vaping.
2Johannesburg, ZASouth Africa's biggest market by volume — Gauteng's malls and townships carry the trade, and Joburg hosts the continent's biggest vape expos.
3Durban, ZAThe third South African hub: port-city supply, a young coastal market, and the same R2.90→R3.29/ml tax regime that reshaped legal retail nationwide.
4Pretoria, ZAGauteng's second pole — civil-service salaries and student populations (Tuks) keep it level with Durban.
5Lagos, NGWest Africa's biggest market: Nigeria has no comprehensive vape law, and cheap Chinese disposables poured into Lagos's informal retail from 2021 — youth surveys in the city show fast-rising experimentation.
6Nairobi, KEKenya regulates vapes under its tobacco law (taxed, 18+) but enforcement is thin; Nairobi's middle-class youth market is East Africa's largest, supplied through Jomo Kenyatta's cargo terminals.
7Accra, GHGhana's FDA classifies vapes as tobacco products; Accra's young professionals drove West Africa's second formal retail scene before disposables undercut it.
8Casablanca, MAMorocco's market grew through tobacconists and gray imports; Casablanca's French-influenced cafe culture adopted vaping early by African standards.
9Kampala, UGUganda debated an outright ban while Kampala's kiosks kept selling — the classic African pattern of law chasing trade.
10Lusaka / HarareZambia and Zimbabwe's capitals round out the list: small formal markets, big informal ones, and the region's tobacco-growing economies watching vaping eat their cigarette future.

Also worth knowing

The continental wildcard is taxation: South Africa's excise (raised every year since 2023) is pushing price-sensitive buyers toward the same illicit networks that captured over half the country's cigarette market — a warning for every finance ministry on the continent. Meanwhile the tobacco industry's pivot (BAT's Johannesburg R&D, heated-tobacco launches) signals where Big Tobacco thinks African nicotine is heading.

Methodology

Methodology: market-research estimates, University of Cape Town youth vaping studies, South African Revenue Service excise policy records, and national regulatory reporting. No city-level use measurement exists on the continent. Rankings are directional estimates.

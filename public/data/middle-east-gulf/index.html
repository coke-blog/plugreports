Drugs in the Gulf States, 2026: the captagon-to-shabu shift

The Middle East stimulant market transformed after the Assad regime fell in December 2024. This tracker compiles what is publicly documented: seizure volumes, the meth surge, where production moved, and what each Gulf state faces. Every figure is sourced and dated. Free to cite; please link back.

What happened: the 2024–2026 shift

For a decade, the Levant’s stimulant of choice was captagon — the amphetamine-family pill industrialized in Syria under the Assad regime, with Saudi Arabia as its biggest market. When the regime fell in December 2024, that industrial base scattered rather than vanished. UNODC counts a minimum of 177 million tablets intercepted across the Arab region in the twelve months to December 2025 — Syria alone reported seizing over 500 million, Lebanon 78 million. The EU Drugs Agency’s 2026 strategic assessment (with EU-ACT) documents production re-forming in Lebanon, Iraq, Sudan, Yemen, Türkiye, Egypt and Kuwait, with European manufacturers (Netherlands, Germany) entering the supply chain.

The second-order effect matters more for users: captagon tablet prices roughly doubled, and UNODC’s World Drug Report 2026 assesses that displaced users shifted to methamphetamine — “shabu” — which is now among the three most-used drugs in Saudi Arabia alongside cannabis and captagon. Iran intercepted 3,663 kg of meth bound for Europe and the Persian Gulf in the first quarter of 2026 alone. The Gulf states — long treated as destinations — are now production and transit nodes in their own right.

Harm-reduction noteMeth and captagon are both stimulants, but they are not interchangeable: meth is longer-acting, more neurotoxic with repeated use, and its market is less predictable. Heat, sleep deprivation and mixing with other stimulants compound the cardiovascular risk. See the methamphetamine profile and captagon profile.

The bigger regional picture

  • Cocaine is arriving too: Gulf ports (Jebel Ali, Jeddah) appear repeatedly in European and Latin American cocaine corridor reporting — the region’s cocaine market is small but growing fast off the same logistics.
  • Pregabalin on illicit markets: UNODC has flagged pregabalin’s growing presence in Middle East illicit drug markets — a prescription gabapentinoid with its own dependence and withdrawal risks. Profile: pregabalin.
  • Khat remains regional baseline: the oldest stimulant economy in the region (Yemen, Somalia, Ethiopia corridor) — profile: khat.
  • Penalty escalation: Kuwait expanded capital punishment for drug offences in December 2025 and Oman in September 2026 — part of a region-wide hardening even as the UAE builds treatment-first pathways (Hisn 80044).

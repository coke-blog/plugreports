Meth prices in Canada, 2026: the cheapest hard drug in the country, city by city

A point of meth can cost less than a coffee in Canada's Prairie cities — the superlab era collapsed prices and supercharged dependence. Reported prices by city, and why cheap is the dangerous part.

How this page works

Meth is the price-collapse story of Canadian drugs. Industrial-scale labs — in Mexico and increasingly inside Canada — produce methamphetamine so cheaply that a dose costs less than a coffee in the hardest-hit cities. That collapse is why Canada's meth map inverts its cocaine map: the smallest Prairie cities post the highest use per capita (Prince Albert, SK carries roughly nine times Toronto's per-capita meth load in wastewater testing). Cheap, ultra-pure, everywhere: the combination removes every natural brake on daily use, and stimulant toxicity deaths more than doubled between 2018 and 2024.

Also worth knowing

Why cheap is dangerous: when a dose costs pocket change, use stops being gated by payday. Dependence that took months at 2015 prices now takes weeks. And because Canadian meth increasingly shares surfaces, scales and supply lines with fentanyl, every bag carries an opioid-contamination lottery for users with no tolerance. Naloxone is free at Canadian pharmacies — carry it.

Methodology

Methodology: ranges compiled from CBSA/RCMP seizure valuations (2025–26), provincial harm-reduction program reporting, media market reporting and user surveys. Figures are directional; plugreports publishes price data solely as harm-reduction and cost-of-addiction information.

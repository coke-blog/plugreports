Top 10 European cities with the most weed use

Europe's weed capitals measured in sewage — Dutch, German and Slovenian cities top the EUDA wastewater ranking, with Barcelona's clubs and Prague's liberal scene close behind.

How this ranking works

Europe has the world's best city-level drug data: the EUDA/SCORE wastewater program tests around 130 cities in 28 countries every year, including the cannabis metabolite THC-COOH. The 2025 campaign found the highest cannabis loads in cities in the Netherlands, Germany and Slovenia — and use spread evenly across the week, unlike the weekend spiking of party drugs. National surveys add a second layer: France, Spain and Czechia post some of the highest lifetime and last-year prevalence in Europe. One caveat: THC-COOH is excreted at low and variable rates, so the wastewater ranking is noisier for cannabis than for other drugs — treat this list as a band.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures weed use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Amsterdam, NLDutch cities top the European wastewater table, and Amsterdam remains the symbolic capital — ~170 coffeeshops plus a tourist market built on five decades of tolerance. Locals' use is high but unremarkable by Dutch standards; the tourists push the sewage numbers up.
2Ljubljana, SIThe data's dark horse: Slovenian cities rank alongside Dutch and German ones at the top of the EUDA THC-COOH table, and Slovenia's national surveys show rising last-year use, concentrated in the young capital.
3Berlin, DEGerman cities show some of Europe's highest wastewater cannabis loads, and Berlin is the epicenter of the country's new legal reality — Germany legalized adult possession and home grow in April 2024, with cannabis social clubs licensed from July 2024.
4Barcelona, ESSpain's private cannabis social clubs — hundreds operate in Barcelona in a legal gray zone — make the city Europe's de-facto weed-tourism capital. Spanish surveys show among the highest last-year use in the EU.
5Prague, CZCzechia consistently posts among Europe's highest prevalence in national surveys, and Prague's famously relaxed scene (possession decriminalized since 2010) makes it a perennial top-five fixture.
6Zurich, CHSwiss cities rank high in wastewater comparisons, and Switzerland is running the world's most advanced legal-sale pilot programs — Zurich's experiment lets registered adults buy regulated cannabis in pharmacies.
7Copenhagen, DKFreetown Christiania's open-air Pusher Street market operated for 50 years until residents tore it up in 2024 — but the demand it served hasn't gone anywhere, and Danish surveys keep Copenhagen in Europe's upper tier.
8Madrid, ESSpain's second club capital, with the country's largest population of users in absolute terms; national surveys put Spanish last-year use among young adults at the top of the EU table.
9Paris, FRFrance has among the highest lifetime cannabis use ever measured in the EU (roughly two in five adults have tried it) despite some of Europe's strictest laws — Paris concentrates that paradox.
10Eindhoven / Tilburg, NLThe Dutch south: border-city coffeeshops serving Belgian, German and French buyers keep wastewater loads high, and Eindhoven hosts part of the Netherlands' regulated-supply experiment.

Also worth knowing

Just outside the top ten: Rome, Brussels, Helsinki (fast-rising), and London (the UK doesn't participate in EUDA wastewater, but national surveys put British use mid-table — with a large illicit market). The big trend is regulation: Germany's legalization, Switzerland's sale pilots, the Dutch supply experiment, and Czechia's 2026 reforms are slowly converting Europe's gray markets into licensed ones.

Methodology

Methodology: EUDA/SCORE wastewater analysis (THC-COOH loads, ~130 cities, 2025 campaign), EMCDDA/EUDA national survey prevalence tables, and national policy reporting. THC-COOH excretion varies between individuals, making cannabis wastewater rankings noisier than other drugs. Rankings are directional.

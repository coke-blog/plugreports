Top African cities for crack cocaine use

Crack's African footprint is small but real — Johannesburg, Cape Town and Lagos lead a market growing on the back of record cocaine transshipment.

GrowingAfrica's cocaine supply hit records — local crack markets follow the transit routes
ZA leadsSouth Africa has the continent's most established crack market ('rocks')
Corridor logiccrack appears where cocaine transships: Lagos, Nairobi, the SA ports

How this ranking works

Africa's crack story follows its cocaine corridors: as the continent became a major transit zone between South America and Europe/Asia, local consumption markets grew along the routes. South Africa has the oldest and largest market (crack 'rocks' have been sold in Cape Town and Johannesburg since the 1990s), with Nigeria, Kenya and Mozambique developing visible markets in transit cities. Data is thin — treat positions as bands.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures crack use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Johannesburg, ZAAfrica's largest crack market — 'rocks' have been entrenched in Joburg's inner city (Hillbrow) since the 1990s; SACENDU reporting consistently ranks smoked cocaine high in treatment admissions.
2Cape Town, ZAThe twin hub — Cape Town's crack market overlaps with its meth ('tik') crisis, and treatment data shows both stimulants at scale.
3Durban, ZAThe port city — cocaine lands here, and the local market follows.
4Lagos, NGWest Africa's hub — Lagos's cocaine market (powder and base) is the region's largest, fed by direct Brazil–Nigeria routes.
5Nairobi, KEEast Africa's transit-to-consumption story — Kenyan seizures have grown, and Nairobi's street markets now include smokable cocaine.
6Maputo, MZMozambique's capital is a key landing point for the 'southern route' — local use has followed transshipment.
7Abidjan, CIWest Africa's secondary hub — Côte d'Ivoire's record seizures (2022's 2-ton bust) track with a growing local market.
8Dakar, SNSenegal's capital sits on the Atlantic transshipment route; local consumption indicators are rising.

Also worth knowing

Context matters: in most of Africa, crack is a secondary market behind the bigger crises — kush in Sierra Leone/Liberia, tramadol in the Sahel and West Africa, meth (tik) in South Africa, nyaope in the townships. But every record cocaine seizure year pushes the local markets up.

Methodology

Methodology: UNODC World Drug Report, SACENDU (South Africa) treatment data, national seizure reporting, ENACT organized-crime research. Rankings are hedged bands.

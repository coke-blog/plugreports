Top 10 African cities with the most weed use

Africa's weed map — Nigeria's world-leading prevalence, South Africa's legal private market, and Morocco's Rif, from Lagos to Cape Town, per UNODC data.

How this ranking works

Africa has the world's fastest-growing cannabis population and some of its least precise data. The landmarks: a 2018 UNODC-supported national survey found Nigeria's past-year prevalence at 10.8% — among the highest ever measured in any country; South Africa's Constitutional Court decriminalized private adult use in 2018 and parliament completed the framework in 2024; and Morocco — the historic supplier of Europe's hashish — legalized medical and industrial cultivation in 2021. City rankings combine those national anchors with treatment and seizure reporting. Every position here is an informed estimate.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures weed use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Lagos, NGThe megacity at the center of the world's highest-prevalence national market: Nigeria's 10.8% past-year rate translates to tens of millions of users, and Lagos — 20+ million people — is both the biggest consumption and the biggest distribution hub in West Africa.
2Johannesburg, ZASouth Africa's commercial capital runs Africa's most formalized weed economy — private clubs and 'dispensary-style' gray-market shops multiplied after the 2018 court ruling, years before the 2024 Act clarified the rules.
3Cape Town, ZAThe counterculture and cultivation capital: the Western Cape's grow regions supply the country, Cape Town's ' cannabis cup' scene is the continent's most visible, and use rates in the metro lead national surveys.
4Chefchaouen / Al Hoceima, MAThe heart of the Rif — towns whose entire economy ran on kif for a century. Legalization (2021) moved production toward licensed cooperatives, but the Rif remains the supply engine for Europe's hashish market.
5Durban, ZAHome of 'Durban Poison' — one of the world's most famous landrace strains — and South Africa's third major consumption market, with KwaZulu-Natal's rural grow regions behind it.
6Nairobi, KEEast Africa's biggest consumer market: Kenyan surveys show rising urban youth use, and Nairobi's 'bhang' trade (plus smuggled Ethiopian and Tanzanian highland product) supplies it.
7Accra, GHGhana passed a limited THC-threshold licensing law in 2020 (Narcotics Control Commission Act) after years of de-facto tolerance; Accra's youth market is West Africa's second most visible after Lagos.
8Abuja / Port Harcourt, NGNigeria's second tier — the federal capital and the oil hub both show high use in the national survey's state breakdowns.
9Addis Ababa, ETEthiopia grows and consumes chat legally, but cannabis ('dagga') use in the capital is documented and climbing, supplied by the country's southern highlands.
10Dakar, SNSenegal's capital anchors a coastal West African consumption belt (Casamance-grown 'yamba'), with use normalized in parts of the music and fishing communities.

Also worth knowing

The continental trend is reform: South Africa (2024), Morocco (2021), Ghana (2020), Zimbabwe and Lesotho (medical/export licensing), Malawi (2020 industrial) and Rwanda have all moved, driven partly by the export economics of medical cannabis. UNODC estimates African users will grow faster than any other region through 2030 — but data quality means every ranking here is approximate.

Methodology

Methodology: UNODC World Drug Report regional estimates, the UNODC-supported Nigeria national drug use survey (2018), South African legal-market reporting, and treatment/seizure data from national drug agencies. No city-level measurement exists on the continent. Rankings are directional estimates.

Top 10 US cities with the most cocaine use

The US cities where cocaine use is highest, ranked from state-level survey data, DEA NFLIS forensic reporting and market intelligence — with plain-English context per city.

How this ranking works

No US agency publishes a clean per-city cocaine ranking. The closest proxies are SAMHSA's state-level past-year use estimates (Colorado, Vermont, Rhode Island, Massachusetts, California, New York and DC lead the country), DEA NFLIS forensic-lab report counts by metro area, and drug-market reporting on distribution hubs. Combined they draw a consistent map: the biggest cocaine markets are the Northeast corridor, South Florida, and the Southwest border metros — with Colorado and the Pacific Northwest as the highest per-capita user states. Positions 4–8 should be read as a band, not an exact order.

#CityWhy it ranks here
1New York City, NYThe largest cocaine market in America by consumption volume. New York State sits in the top tier of past-year use (~2.4% of adults, ~364,000 users), and NYC's nightlife economy, finance-sector demand and port/airport entry points make it the Northeast's undisputed hub.
2Miami, FLThe historical gateway — Miami has been the US cocaine entry point since the 1980s, and South Florida HIDTA reporting still places it among the top import-and-consumption metros, fed by Caribbean and maritime routes.
3Los Angeles, CACalifornia has the largest cocaine user population of any state (~754,000 past-year users), and LA is its center — a huge consumer market that also stages distribution for the whole West.
4Washington, DCThe highest per-capita cocaine use in the country: ~3.7% of DC adults used cocaine in the past year — more than double the national average of 1.76%.
5Chicago, ILThe Midwest's cocaine capital — Illinois posts high per-capita use (~2.2%), and Chicago's role as the region's distribution crossroads keeps its market large and visible in NFLIS metro reporting.
6Houston, TXTexas reports the largest cocaine user base in the South (~318,000), and Houston's port, border proximity and size make it the state's dominant market.
7Denver, COColorado leads the nation in per-capita cocaine use (~3.1% of adults past-year) — Denver's young, affluent, nightlife-heavy demographics drive the country's highest consumption rate outside DC.
8Boston, MAMassachusetts ranks #4 nationally for per-capita use (~2.6%); Boston anchors the New England market, which surveys consistently place in the country's top tier.
9Phoenix, AZSurvey data places Phoenix at or near the top of US cities for lifetime cocaine use (over 20% of residents report having tried it), and the I-10 border corridor keeps supply cheap and constant.
10Atlanta, GAThe Southeast's distribution hub — Georgia's user numbers are lower per capita, but Atlanta's interstate crossroads (I-75/I-85/I-20) make it the region's cocaine logistics center.

Also worth knowing

Honorable mentions: San Francisco/Oakland and Seattle (Pacific states rank high per capita), Las Vegas (tourism-driven market), and Dallas. Note the pattern: cocaine in America is a big-city, weekend drug — unlike meth, it concentrates in the largest metros, not rural areas.

Methodology

Methodology: composite of SAMHSA NSDUH state-level past-year cocaine use estimates (2023–24), DEA NFLIS-Drug forensic report counts by metro area, and HIDTA / drug-market reporting. State-level rates are mapped to their dominant cities. Rankings are directional.

Sources

  • DEA 2025 National Drug Threat Assessment

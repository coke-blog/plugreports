Top 10 US cities with the most vaping

The US cities where vaping is most common — ranked from CDC BRFSS state surveys, FDA enforcement data and market reports, from Oklahoma City's top-rate status to the Appalachian vape belt.

6.7%Oklahoma's adult vaping rate — the highest ever measured in CDC BRFSS state data
1.6MUS middle- and high-school students still reporting current e-cigarette use (FDA/CDC NYTS 2024) — down from a 5.4M peak in 2019
#1e-cigarettes have been the most-used tobacco product among US teens every year since 2014

How this ranking works

No US agency measures vaping city by city, so this ranking triangulates three proxies: CDC BRFSS state-level adult vaping rates (which consistently put Oklahoma, West Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee, Arkansas, Louisiana, Ohio, Nevada, Wyoming and Arizona at the top), FDA/CDC youth survey patterns, and retail-plus-enforcement data. The map they draw is clear: US vaping tracks the old smoking belt — Appalachia and the South — plus Oklahoma's outlier market. City positions within states are estimated from metro populations and retail density; treat the whole list as a band.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures vaping use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Oklahoma City, OKOklahoma recorded the highest adult e-cigarette rate ever measured in BRFSS state data (6.7% current use, 7.9% among men) — and OKC, with weak retail restrictions and cheap disposables, is the market's center of gravity.
2Tulsa, OKShares Oklahoma's nation-leading numbers; state survey breakdowns put Tulsa's metro essentially level with OKC.
3Charleston / Huntington, WVWest Virginia is America's smoking capital (roughly one in four adults in older BRFSS waves) and vaping followed the same map — the state's young-adult vaping rates are among the nation's highest, concentrated in its two small metros.
4Louisville / Lexington, KYKentucky pairs top-tier smoking with top-tier vaping in federal survey data, and its two cities anchor the state's market. Louisville's vape-shop density is among the highest in the South.
5Nashville / Memphis, TNTennessee appears in the top vaping tier of every BRFSS wave, and Nashville's boomtown demographics (young, nightlife-heavy) amplify it.
6New Orleans, LALouisiana rounds out the Southern top tier; New Orleans' 24-hour hospitality economy drives both cigarette and vape volume well above state averages.
7Indianapolis, INIndiana sits in the second-highest vaping tier nationally; Indianapolis is the Midwest's biggest vape market after Columbus, with high youth uptake in state surveys.
8Columbus, OHOhio posts some of the highest female adult vaping rates in BRFSS data; Columbus's huge student population (Ohio State) concentrates the state's young-adult use.
9Las Vegas, NVNevada's top-tier vaping rate meets the tourist economy — the Strip's vape and smoke shops do volumes that skew the state's per-capita numbers upward.
10Phoenix / Tucson, AZArizona holds a consistent spot in the top ten states for adult vaping; Phoenix's size makes it the Southwest's largest vape market.

Also worth knowing

The paradox cities: San Francisco — birthplace of Juul — banned e-cigarette retail sales in 2019, and New York, Chicago and Boston run flavor bans, yet their illicit and border markets keep big-city use alive. The national story is decline among teens (from 5.4 million users in 2019 to ~1.6 million in 2024) but persistence among young adults, driven by flavored disposables the FDA has largely failed to keep off shelves.

Methodology

Methodology: CDC BRFSS state adult e-cigarette prevalence (most recent comparable waves), FDA/CDC National Youth Tobacco Survey, state health-department surveys, and FDA enforcement/retail data. No city-level use survey exists; city ranks within top states are estimated. Rankings are directional.

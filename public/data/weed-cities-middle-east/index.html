Top 10 Middle Eastern cities with the most weed use

Weed in the Middle East — Israel's high-prevalence cities, Lebanon's hashish heartland in the Bekaa, and the region's slow shift from prohibition to medical programs.

Top-tierIsrael posts among the highest national past-year cannabis prevalence measured anywhere (national surveys)
2020Lebanon became the first Arab country to legalize cannabis cultivation (medical/industrial)
BekaaLebanon's Bekaa Valley is one of the world's oldest and largest hashish-producing regions

How this ranking works

No Middle Eastern country publishes city-level cannabis data, and most governments underreport use — so this ranking leans on three proxies: Israel's unusually good national survey data, UNODC World Drug Report country estimates, and the production geography of hashish (Lebanon's Bekaa Valley, with Turkish and Egyptian transit and consumption markets). The headline: the Middle East's weed capital is Tel Aviv by use, Baalbek by production. Everywhere else, harsh laws push the market underground — treat this entire list as an informed estimate.

Read this firstNo country anywhere measures weed use per city directly. This ranking combines the strongest available proxies — wastewater analysis, forensic-lab reporting, treatment admissions and police seizures — and is directional, not a precise league table. Positions in the middle of each list should be read as a band.
#CityWhy it ranks here
1Tel Aviv, ILIsrael's national surveys put past-year cannabis use at the top of international comparisons (around one in four adults in some waves), and Tel Aviv — young, secular, nightlife-dense — concentrates it. Israel also runs one of the world's largest medical cannabis programs (~100,000 licensed patients).
2Baalbek / Hermel, LBThe capital of the Bekaa Valley's hashish industry — thousands of hectares of cannabis cultivated openly in defiance of, then under the umbrella of, Lebanon's 2020 cultivation law. Production, not consumption, ranks it here.
3Beirut, LBLebanon's consumption capital: hash is cheap, locally produced and socially normalized across much of Beirut despite formal prohibition, and the economic collapse made it cheaper still.
4Jerusalem / Haifa, ILIsrael's other major cities track Tel Aviv's national-level prevalence a notch lower; Haifa's port keeps supply easy.
5Istanbul, TRTürkiye is a transit corridor for Afghan hashish and a producer in its own southeast; Istanbul's 16 million people make it the region's largest absolute consumer market, with use concentrated in nightlife districts.
6Tehran, IRIran's drug data focuses on opiates, but hashish flows west from Afghanistan through the capital; treatment-system reporting shows cannabis the most-used illicit drug after opiates among young Tehranis.
7Cairo, EGEgypt's hashish market (supplied via the Sinai and Sudan routes) is centuries old and enormous in absolute terms; 'bango' (local herbal cannabis) is grown in Upper Egypt and the Sinai.
8Amman, JOJordan is mostly a transit country (Captagon north, hashish south), but Amman's young, urban population drives the region's second tier of consumption.
9Dubai, AETiny prevalence but wealthy demand — the Gulf's strictest laws (residue-level prosecutions, though softened in 2021) suppress a market that persists among expatriates and the party circuit.
10Ramallah / West BankPalestinian surveys are sparse, but hashish use in the West Bank is documented and rising, supplied through the same Israeli market that tops this list.

Also worth knowing

The structural trend: medical programs are the thin end of the wedge. Israel's is huge, Lebanon legalized cultivation (2020), and Türkiye legalized medical production — while the Gulf states keep near-zero tolerance. UNODC estimates for the region are among the world's least reliable; every ranking here should be read as approximate.

Methodology

Methodology: Israeli national drug surveys and medical-program licensing data, UNODC World Drug Report country estimates, Lebanese cultivation-law reporting, and regional seizure/transit analysis. No city-level measurement exists anywhere in the region. Rankings are directional estimates.

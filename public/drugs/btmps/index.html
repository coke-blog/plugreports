BTMPS (Tinuvin 770)

Street names: Tinuvin 770, HALS-770, Bis(2,2,6,6-tetramethyl-4-piperidyl) sebacate

Overdose? Act now. Call emergency services — say "unresponsive, not breathing". Give naloxone for opioid-like signs. Hotlines

⚡What it does

  • No psychoactive effects of its own at relevant doses — people take it unknowingly inside fentanyl
  • Acts as a CYP enzyme inhibitor, slowing the body's breakdown of fentanyl and extending its sedating effects for hours beyond the norm
  • The result: overdose symptoms that outlast naloxone's window

⚠Key risks

  • Identified in 2024 as the cutting agent behind mass-overdose clusters in the US Midwest — multiple naloxone-resistant-appearing overdoses traced to the same fentanyl batch
  • The 'no naloxone response' pattern isn't because naloxone fails — it's because the sedative keeps being re-released as the body slowly clears the mix; repeat dosing and rescue breathing are essential
  • Industrial-grade material carries unknown impurities; chronic effects of inhaling or injecting a plastic stabilizer are unstudied
  • Its presence is invisible — users can't taste, see or test for it with fentanyl strips
  • A preview of the future: as cutting agents evolve, overdose clusters will increasingly involve non-opioid chemistry that naloxone can't touch

✚Overdose signs

  • Sedation that repeatedly returns after naloxone wears off (30–90 minutes) — expect re-dosing
  • Prolonged unresponsiveness with slow breathing lasting many hours
  • Clusters of simultaneous overdoses from the same batch — treat as contaminated supply, alert others

Schedule / classNot a controlled substance — an industrial chemical with no legitimate human use
AppearanceWhite crystalline granules or powder, sold legitimately as a light stabilizer for plastics and coatings; in drug supplies it's an invisible powder cut into fentanyl
Street priceLegitimate industrial chemical ~$5–15/kg — worth pennies per cut dose
Legal statusUnscheduled and legal to buy as an industrial chemical; its presence in drug supply is an adulteration issue, not a possession one (though drug delivery charges apply where such laws exist).
